# IEEE General Paper Writing Guidelines

## 1. Abstract
*   **Purpose**: A standalone summary of the paper.
*   **Content**:
    *   **Objective**: What is the problem?
    *   **Method**: How did you solve it?
    *   **Results**: What did you find? (Include key metrics).
    *   **Conclusion**: What is the impact?
*   **Length**: Typically 150–250 words.
*   **Constraint**: No citations, no undefined acronyms, no equations.

## 2. Introduction
*   **Structure**: Funnel approach (Broad context → Specific problem → Proposed solution).
*   **Key Elements**:
    *   **Motivation**: Why is this topic important?
    *   **Problem Statement**: What is the specific gap or challenge?
    *   **Existing Solutions**: Briefly mention why current methods fail (leading to your contribution).
    *   **Contribution**: Explicitly list your contributions (e.g., "The main contributions of this paper are...").
    *   **Organization**: Roadmap of the rest of the paper.

## 3. Methodology (Proposed System)
*   **Clarity**: Must be reproducible. A reader should be able to rebuild your system based on this section.
*   **Visuals**: Use block diagrams, flowcharts, and algorithms.
*   **Formalism**: Use mathematical notation where appropriate to define problems or algorithms precisely.

## 4. Results and Discussion
*   **Evidence**: Claims made in the introduction must be backed by evidence here.
*   **Comparison**: Compare your results with state-of-the-art (SOTA) or baseline methods.
*   **Visuals**: Use graphs (not just tables) to show trends. Ensure axes are labeled and legends are clear.
*   **Analysis**: Don't just list numbers; explain *why* the results are good or bad. Discuss trade-offs (e.g., accuracy vs. speed).

## 5. Conclusion
*   **Summary**: Recap the problem and solution.
*   **Key Findings**: Reiterate the most important results.
*   **Future Work**: Suggest 2-3 directions for future research.
*   **Constraint**: Do not simply copy-paste the Abstract.

## 6. Writing Style & Tone
*   **Voice**: Use the third person (e.g., "The system was tested..." instead of "I tested...").
*   **Tense**:
    *   Use *past tense* for work done (e.g., "We measured...").
    *   Use *present tense* for established facts (e.g., "Python is a programming language...").
*   **Clarity**: Short, direct sentences are better than long, complex ones.
*   **Acronyms**: Define on first use (e.g., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)").

## 7. Ethical Considerations
*   **Plagiarism**: Ensure all text is original. Paraphrase and cite correctly.
*   **Data Fabrication**: Never invent or massage data to fit a hypothesis.
*   **Authorship**: Only include authors who made a significant contribution.

## 8. References
*   **Quality**: Prefer peer-reviewed sources (Journals, Conferences) over web pages or white papers.
*   **Recency**: Ensure a significant portion of references are from the last 3-5 years.
*   **Format**: Strictly follow the IEEE citation style (see `ieee_format_and_guidelines.md`).
